Ship reviews that help you choose

Most ship reviews are press releases. Here's what to actually look for — especially if the casino is part of your trip.

Casino conditions

This is the section other reviewers skip and you shouldn't. Note the size of the casino, the table game mix and minimums at different hours, how many machines are actually maintained, and whether the casino feels like an afterthought or a real venue. Smoking policy matters enormously — a smoking casino changes the whole atmosphere. Ask what the host situation is: a good casino host team is the difference between being rated fairly and being invisible.

Cabin comfort

Ignore the square footage number. What matters: bed comfort, shower pressure and size, storage for a long sailing, outlet count and placement, noise from hallways and above-deck venues, and balcony usability (wind, soot, privacy). On a 29-day sailing, a bad cabin is not a minor annoyance — it's your home.

Food, honestly

Separate the main dining room (consistency over two weeks matters more than one great night), the buffet (crowd flow at peak times), and specialty restaurants (worth it or not, with prices). Note embarkation-day lunch and port-day breakfast — the meals reviewers never mention that you'll eat constantly.

Wi-Fi and work

Test real speeds, not the marketed ones. Can you video call? Upload photos? Note dead zones and whether packages are per-device or per-cabin. If you work from the ship, this section decides your sailing.

Crowds and vibe

Who is this ship for? Party crowd, retirees, families, or a mix — and does that match you? Note sea-day pool chair competition, show seating, and whether the ship feels full at double occupancy. Demographics aren't a judgment; fit is everything.

The one-line verdict format

Every review should end with: who this ship is for, who should skip it, and the one thing that would change your mind. If a review can't answer those three, it told you nothing.
