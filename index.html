The free cruise playbook

How casino comps actually become cruises

A comp is a marketing offer based on expected play—not a prize and not truly free. The smart move is to understand how casinos value your action, compare the full trip cost, and never gamble more just to “earn” an offer.

Play is rated

Casinos look at game type, average bet, time played, and their mathematical edge—not just whether you won or lost.

Offers have layers

The cabin may be covered while taxes, port fees, gratuities, travel, and onboard spending remain yours.

Comparison wins

A discounted fare with better terms can beat a “free” cabin once every required cost is included.

Start with the programs you already use

Join the loyalty club before playing. Keep your card active at tables and machines so the activity you choose is actually tracked.

Ask how your play is measured

For table games, confirm the rating after a session. For slots, use your card consistently. Don’t assume the system caught everything.

Connect land and sea offers

Some cruise programs match or review land-casino offers; casino hosts and specialized travel advisors can help identify legitimate paths.

Price the complete trip

Add taxes, port charges, deposits, gratuities, transportation, hotel nights, and any minimum-play expectation before saying yes.

Protect your bankroll

Set a gambling budget independent of the offer. A cabin is never worth increasing play beyond what you can comfortably lose.

Document the terms

Save the offer, booking confirmation, cabin category, expiration date, cancellation rules, and what happens if the casino changes the sailing.

Honest rule: if you would not have played anyway, the comp is probably the most expensive way to buy the trip. Responsible play comes first.
